That’s what I got when I opened my Facebook at the weekend to find an estate agent advert from Robert Ellis Estate Agents for one of the first projects I worked on at Allan Joyce Architects after completing my Part 2 qualification.

The project was for a new contemporary rear extension, first floor side extension to match the existing architectural style, and internal redesign of a 4 bedroom detached interwar house on the prestigious Beeston Fields Drive, Beeston, Nottingham. This was a great project to work on and was the first time I had been given full design and management responsibilities. There were a lot of learning opportunities, such as keeping the contemporary design of the new extension sympathetic to the original house, the structural problem of putting a second storey onto existing shallow foundations, and design of the new leaded entrance canopy.

The rear extension allowed space for an open plan kitchen, dining and living room, which is sought by most clients who want a more sociable space for family or entertaining guests. The ground floor is expansive with multiple reception rooms, so even with the open plan there are still snug spaces to retreat to. This is an approach that can be taken for smaller houses too, even a humble two-up-two-down has a ‘spare’ room that can become a snug following a new open plan extension.

The slimline sliding patio doors from Smart Aluminium Systems (https://www.smartsystems.co.uk/) helped to create a panoramic view of the garden and golf course grounds beyond. I’ll always recommend sliding doors over bi-fold doors as they have less frame and therefore optimise the views. The patio doors were designed with a level threshold out onto the Indian sandstone patio with a discreet slot drain to prevent water getting in.

The first floor extension increased allowed for a two ensuite bedroom ‘guest suite’ that increased the total number of bedrooms to five. At the start of the project, we got a contractor and structural engineer on site to investigate trial holes and determine the build up of the existing foundation and whether they could take the structural load of a new storey. This alongside whether the new extension foundations would undermine the existing.

The house also had new 200 Series windows throughout from Velfac (https://velfac.co.uk/windows/) The design of these windows is simple with a slimline frame and suits the 1930s house – short of painstakingly crafting timber windows with leaded lights, this option was a close second. The client chose a RAL colour to match the Farrow and Ball colour ‘String’ that complimented the tone of the bricks and mortar beautifully.

Unfortunately, the sale of the house is overshadowed by the recent sale of the neighbouring golf club and a local petition to oppose any new housing developments on the site. One can only hope that the silver lining is that any new homes built meet local needs – Beeston is an area of Nottingham that is only becoming more expensive, and the reality is that there needs to be more affordable and social housing in the area to cope with demand and allow those renting locally, or children moving out of the family home, to remain in the area.
